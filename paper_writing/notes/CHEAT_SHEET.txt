                    PATHSENSE PRESENTATION — ONE-PAGE CHEAT SHEET
                              June 2, 2026 | 5 Minutes
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

KEY NUMBERS TO MEMORIZE:
  • 25 participants (3 groups, ~8-9 each)
  • 4 difficulty levels
  • Practice round: Haptic 4.88 falls vs Audio 0.33 falls = 10× difference
  • Bayes factor: BF₀₁ ≈ 84 (strong evidence for null)

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
STORY ARC (Remember this):
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

1. PROBLEM: Off-hand tilt feels awkward (why?)
2. INSIGHT: Body schema—phone needs to give feedback
3. QUESTION: Which feedback works best? (haptic vs audio vs none)
4. DESIGN: PathSense—tightrope game, 3 groups, 4 levels
5. DATA: All groups follow same learning curve (no winner)
6. DISCOVERY: Baseline wasn't equal! (10× difference pre-feedback)
7. IMPLICATION: Baseline control matters—can't ignore it
8. WHO BENEFITS: Gamers w/ motor challenges, older adults, one-handed users
9. NEXT: Scale up, test adults, better haptics, real-world testing

IF SOMEONE ASKS A QUESTION:
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Q: "Why didn't any modality win?"
A: "Because we didn't control for baseline. The real finding is that baseline
   control matters. That's what future studies need to account for."

Q: "Isn't a null result a failure?"
A: "No—null results force us to ask harder questions. The blueprint we built
   from this study is actually more valuable than a simple 'haptic wins' result."

Q: "How does this apply to real games?"
A: "Right now games don't measure baseline. Our work says: measure first, THEN
   add feedback. Especially for accessibility—gamers with motor challenges need
   that baseline assessment."
