# Two Key Figures Explained Simply
## PathSense Study — Slide 8

---

## **Figure 1: Learning Curve (Left Panel)**

### **What It Shows:**
**How many times each group falls as the game gets harder**

### **The X-Axis (Difficulty):**
- Practice → Easy → Medium → Hard
- (4 levels, getting progressively harder)

### **The Y-Axis (Falls):**
- How many times the ball fell off the path
- Lower = better performance

### **What You See:**
Three lines (one for each modality: Haptic, Audio, None)

### **The Story:**

**At Practice & Easy levels:** ✓ All groups improve
- They're learning! Falls go DOWN
- This is normal — people get better quickly

**At Medium level:** ⚠️ Starting to struggle
- Falls increase a bit
- Getting harder

**At Hard level:** 💥 Everyone crashes
- ALL THREE LINES GO UP TOGETHER
- All groups fall a lot
- No group is better than the other

### **The Key Finding:**
**Difficulty beats modality.** When the game got hard, feedback type didn't matter. Everyone struggled the same way.

### **Simple Explanation:**
*"You see how all three lines follow the same pattern? They all go down, then up together? That tells us difficulty is the real limiting factor, not whether you got haptic, audio, or no feedback."*

---

## **Figure 2: Time-Locked Correction (Right Panel)**

### **What It Shows:**
**When the phone warns you (proximity gets closer), do you steer back toward the path?**

### **The Setup:**
We looked at every moment when the phone said: **"Warning! You're getting close to the edge!"**

Then we asked: **"What did players do in the 3 seconds after that warning?"**

### **The X-Axis (Time):**
- 0 seconds = the exact moment the warning fired
- Negative (−1 to 0 sec) = before the warning
- Positive (0 to +3 sec) = after the warning
- Vertical dashed line = the warning moment

### **The Y-Axis:**
**Left panel:** How close to the edge is the ball now? (proximity level)
**Right panel:** How much is the player tilting? (tilt magnitude)

### **What You See:**
Three lines again (Haptic, Audio, None)

### **The Story:**

**Before the warning (−1 to 0 sec):**
- All groups at the same "closeness to edge"
- Then BAM—proximity escalates (dashed line)

**After the warning (0 to +3 sec):**
- Do all groups steer back equally?
- Do some recover faster?

### **What We Found:**
**All three lines follow the same pattern.** They all have similar recovery times and similar tilt corrections.

### **The Key Finding:**
**No modality advantage.** When warned, people steer back similarly whether they got haptic, audio, or nothing.

### **Simple Explanation:**
*"The time-locked figure shows that when the phone warned players they were getting close, all three groups reacted the same way and recovered at the same speed. Modality didn't change how fast they could correct."*

---

## **Together: What Both Figures Say**

| Figure | Finding | Meaning |
|--------|---------|---------|
| **Learning Curve** | All groups follow same pattern | Difficulty dominates, not modality |
| **Time-Locked** | All groups recover same speed | Feedback modality doesn't change reaction |
| **Both together** | Modality has no effect anywhere | On learning, on immediate response, nowhere |

## **If Someone Asks:**

**Q: "Why time-locked?"**
A: "We 'locked' each warning event to time zero, then averaged across all warnings. That way we could see if people reacted differently to the same warning type."

**Q: "What are the shaded bands?"**
A: "That's the uncertainty (±SE). Shows where typical players landed."

**Q: "Why does it matter that all three lines are similar?"**
A: "Because if haptic was better, the blue line would drop faster or stay lower. But it doesn't. All three groups respond the same way."

## **The Slide 8 Story (Both Panels Together):**

### **Left Panel (Learning Curve):**
"As the game gets harder, everyone improves, then everyone crashes. No group wins."

### **Right Panel (Time-Locked Response):**
"When warned, everyone steers back at the same speed. No group wins again."

### **Conclusion:**
"Modality doesn't predict performance. Task difficulty does."
