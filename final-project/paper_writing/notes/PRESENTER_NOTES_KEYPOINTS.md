## **SLIDE 6: FALLS & CONTROL** (30 sec)
**Look at both graphs:**
- "Left: fall counts. Right: path deviation."

**The result:**
- "All three groups look the same. Statistically: p > 0.19."

**The statistics:**
- "Bayes factors (BF₀₁ ≈ 84) show strong evidence for NO effect."
- "Effect sizes: r = −0.44 to +0.25 (negligible)."
- "Sample sizes: n = 8–9 per group."

**Translate:**
- "We tested this thoroughly. There's no modality winner."

---

## **SLIDE 7: BASELINE CONFOUND** (40 sec)
**Left panel — point to the numbers:**
- "Audio baseline: 0.33 falls" ✓
- "Haptic baseline: 4.88 falls" ✗
- "That's 10× difference."

**Key insight:**
- "Groups weren't equal from the start. This matters."

**Statistical detail:**
- "Mann-Whitney U = 28, p = 0.04, r = −0.57."

**What this means:**
- "Haptic improved −1.38. Audio flat. None improved −2.38."
- "But baseline was so different, raw comparisons are misleading."

**Right panel — Proximity Response:**
- "All groups respond to warnings similarly."
- "Modalities: Haptic (blue) | Audio (red) | None (gray)"

---

## **SLIDE 8: LEARNING & STEERING** (30 sec)
**Learning Curve (left):**
- "All groups: improve at Easy, crash at Hard."
- "Same pattern for all three."

**Steering Response (right):**
- "When warned, all groups tilt back at the same speed."
- "Time-locked to the warning moment (t=0)."

**Key takeaway:**
- "Difficulty dominates. Modality has no interaction with learning."

---

## **SLIDE 9: WHAT WE ACTUALLY FOUND** (45 sec)
**Three findings:**

1. **Baseline varies 10×**
   - "Before any feedback, groups were different."

2. **Modality has no effect**
   - "p > 0.19, BF₀₁ ≈ 84 (strong null evidence)."

3. **Difficulty dominates**
   - "All groups hit the same ceiling at Hard level."

**Participant quote:**
- "One person said: 'It's good training for elders or students with special needs.'"
- But we can't yet explain why.

**Next step:**
- "We need individual-level analysis to understand who responds to what."

---

## **SLIDE 10: CONCLUSION** (30 sec)
**Summary:**
- RQ1: No modality effect
- RQ2/RQ3: Baseline responsiveness matters more
- RQ4: Task difficulty dominates

**Reframe the null:**
- "This study said No to modality."
- "But that No is valuable—it tells us to look elsewhere."
- "Individual differences, not technology choice."

**References:**
- Guiard (1987) | Maravita & Iriki (2004) | Cuppone et al. (2016)

**Final close:**
- "Baseline matters. Measure it first. Then customize."

## **IF ASKED QUESTIONS:**

**Q: "Why didn't haptic win?"**
A: "Baseline confound—haptic group started 10× worse. When we account for that, no clear winner."

**Q: "Is this a failure?"**
A: "No. Bayes factors (BF₀₁ ≈ 84) show strong evidence the null is real, not just low power."

**Q: "What should designers do?"**
A: "Measure baseline first. Don't assume feedback solves it. Task difficulty is the limiter."

**Q: "Why focus on non-dominant hand?"**
A: "It's the hardest case. If we can make off-hand tilt accessible, we've solved the harder problem."
